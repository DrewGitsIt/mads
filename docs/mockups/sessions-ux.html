#2 Rail cards — three ways to harmonize the surfaces every option drops the checkbox gutter, the always-on kebab, and the Resume button

The problem today: each card carries a hidden-but-space-reserving checkbox (the blank left gutter), a persistent ⋯ kebab, a hover Resume button, and a whole-row click target — four surfaces for maybe three real flows (open, resume, remove; link/unlink are flag-gated extras). All three options below collapse to one primary surface (the card = open) and push the rare/destructive actions to a single secondary surface.

Option A · Quiet card
The card is the only chrome. Click to open; opening a paused session resumes it. One faint ⋯ appears on hover for the rare secondary actions — and the same menu is on right-click anywhere in the card. Nothing competes for the eye at rest.

My lean: A for the resting-state calm you asked for, borrowing B's "click resumes a paused session" rule (which is what lets us delete the Resume button outright) and keeping a right-click menu as the power path. C is the most discoverable but reintroduces per-card icon chrome — the very thing #2 is trying to shed. Whichever we pick, the left checkbox gutter is gone: linking becomes a header-level mode or a right-click action, so a single card never reserves space for it.

#3 Link mode flow — Option C three-step experience create leader/monitor relationships between sessions

How it works: click the link icon (🔗) on a card → select other sessions in the same workspace → choose a preset (Monitor or Leader) and confirm who leads → subjects appear nested under their holder in the rail. Data model mirrors railLinks.ts (grants → roles) and LinkChooserDialog.tsx (preset chooser + holder picker).

Step 1 · Link mode initiated

Click the 🔗 icon. The card becomes the anchor; same-workspace cards show checkboxes; cross-workspace cards dim with a tag. A floating bar tracks selection.
